## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**Does this look AI-generated? No.** This reads as a real, opinionated product made by someone with taste. A visitor asks "who built this?" not "which model made this?"

**LLM assessment:** The warm coral is genuinely *committed*, not a timid accent. It drenches Value, the Pricing offer panel, the Steps tiles, the FinalCTA, and the brand mark, easily carrying 30-60%+ of memorable surface. Even the neutrals are hue-tinted toward coral and the shadows are warm, not gray. The font pairing (Bricolage Grotesque display + Hanken Grotesk body) is a deliberate, non-reflex choice. The Showcase mini-sites are the smartest move on the page: three bespoke fake businesses with distinct palettes AND distinct typefaces, which rebuts the "$5 template" fear by demonstration rather than claim. That is design-as-argument. The one genuine slop-adjacent flag is **gradient text** on the hero line "Win more customers." (`background-clip:text`, an absolute ban). Tasteful and small, but a banned tell.

Important caveat: the detector only regex-scans markup, so CSS-Module-expressed patterns are out of scope. It did **not** catch the hero gradient text (that rule lives in `index.css` / a CSS Module, never passed to the scanner). So this clean pass means *no false positives*, not *no issues*. Trust the LLM review over the scanner here.

**Visual overlays:** No user-visible overlay was injected. Browser overlay was skipped (no browser-automation tool in this run). ## Overall Impression

This is a strong, warm, genuinely on-brand page that executes the hardest part of the brief (a truly committed brand color, "Friendly Storefront") correctly. What works: the coral commitment, the Showcase-as-proof, and copy that respects a non-technical audience. What doesn't: the single most important click on the page (Get started) currently goes nowhere, and the "showy" animated layer is quietly fighting the brand's "calm, never showy" positioning. The single biggest opportunity: wire the primary CTA to a real next step, then dial the motion back from "alive at idle" to "alive on intent" so the page feels as quietly confident as its copy.

## What's Working

1. **The committed coral is the whole brand argument.** Most "warm" briefs get a timid coral accent on a beige page. Here coral owns entire sections, neutrals are hue-tinted toward it, and shadows are warm rather than gray. This is the "Friendly Storefront" north star executed correctly, and it is the hardest thing in the brief to get right.
2. **Showcase mini-sites that prove "not a template."** Three distinct businesses with distinct palettes and distinct fonts (serif coffee shop vs sans salon vs sans plumber), each with browser chrome and real-feeling copy. This converts the page's biggest objection ("I'll get a cheap cookie-cutter site") into evidence.
3. **Copy that respects the audience.** Every line is plain, warm, and benefit-led without hype. No countdown timers, no fake scarcity. "Not happy in your first 30 days? Full refund, no hard feelings." Consumer-warm lane landed precisely. (No em dashes in copy, ban-compliant.)

## Priority Issues

**[P1] The primary CTA goes nowhere.**
- **Why it matters:** This is the entire point of the page. All conversion CTAs are `href="#start"` which scrolls to the FinalCTA, whose own "Get started" is `href="#top"` (back to hero). A skeptical, time-poor owner who works up the nerve to click is silently scrolled and dead-ended, puncturing the trust the page worked hard to build at the exact moment of commitment. Violates Error Prevention and Visibility of Status.
- **Fix:** Wire the CTAs to a real next step: a `#start` section with a short capture (business name + email) or a clear "Book a 10-min call." At minimum, stop the circular `#start` -> `#top` loop.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable craft` (build the start/capture step), then `/impeccable harden`.

**[P1] Motion density contradicts the "calm, never showy" brand principle.**
- **Why it matters:** Running concurrently at idle: two drifting aurora blobs, a 7s hero float, magnetic pull on 5+ CTAs, 3D tilt + glare on every showcase card and the hero, hero parallax, Steps scroll-scale, a Pricing sheen sweep, floating Sparkle glyphs in FinalCTA, and the marquee. Each is tasteful; the sum is busy. For a persona who fears "complicated," too much movement subconsciously reads as "flashy = expensive = the thing I'm afraid of." It is the one place the redesign fights its own positioning.
- **Fix:** Cut the most ornamental layer (the FinalCTA floating Sparkles are pure decoration), and run either the hero aurora-drift OR the float, not both. Keep magnetic CTAs and scroll choreography. Aim for "alive on intent," not "alive at idle."
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable quieter`.

**[P2] Steps section is disproportionately long for 3 simple ideas.**
- **Why it matters:** Each step gets a `min-height: clamp(520px, 72vh, 720px)` pin zone; three stack to ~2000px+ of scroll for "tell us / we build / go live." Time-poor mobile users may bail mid-section, before Pricing (the section that would close them). The journey's only real valley.
- **Fix:** Reduce pin-zone height (e.g. `clamp(420px, 56vh, 560px)`) or tighten the cadence so three steps feel like one quick promise. Mobile already drops the pin (good).
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`.

**[P2] Gradient text on the hero accent line is a banned tell and a contrast risk.**
- **Why it matters:** "Win more customers." uses `.accent-gradient` (`background-clip:text`), an absolute ban. The detector missed it (CSS-only), but it is a real slop tell, and gradient text against cream is harder to guarantee at AA at the lighter end.
- **Fix:** Make solid `--coral-strong` the default (it already falls back to solid under reduced-motion). A solid accent line is more confident and on-brand than a gradient. Verify `--coral-strong` clears AA large-text 3:1 on `--cream`.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish` (or `/impeccable colorize`).

**[P3] No scroll-spy / active nav state.**
- **Why it matters:** Nav links only show the coral underline on hover, never the current section. Minor wayfinding gap on a long single-scroll page.
- **Fix:** IntersectionObserver-driven active state on nav links matching the in-view section.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`.

## Persona Red Flags

**Non-technical SMB owner, on a phone (the core target):** The dead-end CTA is the worst failure for exactly this person. She is the most likely to lose nerve, and tapping "Get started" -> scroll -> another "Get started" -> scroll-to-top reads as "broken" and confirms her "this'll be a headache" fear. The long Steps section risks her bailing before Pricing. Positives: mobile hero is clean at 500px, copy is reassuring, pricing is unmissable, which makes the dead-end sting more.

**Skeptical "been burned before" owner (price/trust-sensitive):** Mostly delighted. One honest price, money-back guarantee under the CTA, cancel anytime, no contracts. The page is built for her. Risk: the busy motion may pattern-match to "expensive agency that'll upsell me." Calm copy says approachable; busy motion whispers pricey. Testimonials are initials-only avatars; for maximum trust she would want to believe these are real people.

**Accessibility / reduced-motion user (keyboard + screen reader):** Genuinely well served: skip link, full `prefers-reduced-motion` handling, focus-visible styling, ARIA on carousel and accordion, marquee has a visually-hidden readable list. Watch: testimonial autoplay (6.5s) has no explicit pause button (it does fully pause under reduced-motion). Verify dimmed coral text on deep-coral panels (`--on-coral-dim` on `--coral-deep`) clears AA. The Counter hides the numeric value from AT (`aria-hidden`); the number ("5 days") is arguably meaningful content worth exposing.

## Minor Observations

- `scroll-padding-top: 88px` vs sticky header 72px, and 96-100px offsets in Steps/FAQ: inconsistent anchor clearance. Harmless.
- Nav "Sign in" links to `#pricing`: odd target, reads as placeholder.
- Footer links and social icons all point to `#top` (placeholders): expected for a comp, but non-functional Terms/Privacy/Cookies matters if it ships.
- Marquee pauses on hover but has no keyboard/touch equivalent.
- `KazoomMark` hardcodes `fill="var(--coral-strong)"`, so the mark can't recolor on coral surfaces if ever needed.

## Questions to Consider

1. If you deleted half the motion, would anyone miss it, or would the brand actually feel *more* confident? "Quietly confident" brands often signal confidence through stillness.
2. The page's #1 job is to make a scared owner click "Get started" and actually start. Why does that click currently do nothing?
3. The Showcase proves "not a template" better than any copy could. Why is it buried below the marquee instead of being the second thing you see?
4. Three steps take 2000px of scroll. Is the choreography earning that real estate, or spending the time-poor user's patience to show off a scroll trick?
5. You avoided every loud-hard-sell anti-reference, but is the page too gentle? There is no single moment of "here's exactly why your Facebook-page-as-website is costing you customers."
