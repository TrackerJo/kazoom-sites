# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Small business owners and SMB operators (local services, shops, studios, solo founders) who need a credible web presence but are not designers or developers. They arrive skeptical and time-poor, often unsure whether a "real" website is worth the cost or effort. Their context is practical: they want to look legitimate to customers, be found, and not get burned by something cheap or complicated. Decisions are personal and budget-conscious, frequently made on a phone between other tasks.

## Product Purpose

Kazoom sells websites and web presence to small businesses. This is the marketing/landing surface where a skeptical owner decides Kazoom is trustworthy and worth it. Success is the visitor believing two things: "this will make my business look legitimate" and "I can actually do this without it being a headache." The page itself is the strongest proof of the product, so it must be the kind of site a customer would be proud to own.

## Brand Personality

Warm, approachable, and quietly confident. Three words: friendly, trustworthy, capable. The voice talks to a busy human, not a procurement team: plain, encouraging, and honest about what they get. It lowers the barrier for non-technical owners without ever feeling juvenile or salesy. Closest reference lane is consumer-warm (Mailchimp, Notion): human, soft, and inviting, but credible enough to hand money to.

## Anti-references

- **Cheap template / clip-art.** The single biggest thing to avoid. No generic stock icons, no busy clutter, no "$5 theme" feel. Every element should read as considered and trustworthy, because untrustworthy is the exact fear we are overcoming.
- Cold corporate SaaS: navy gradients, stock office photography, "enterprise" distance.
- Loud, hard-sell marketing: countdown timers, popups, neon urgency.
- Sterile minimalism so stripped-back it reads as cold or unfinished.

## Design Principles

- **Be the proof.** Kazoom builds websites, so this site is the demo. If it does not look like something a small business would be proud to own, nothing else matters. Show, don't tell.
- **Warm authority.** Friendly enough that a non-technical owner feels welcome; polished enough that they trust it with their money. Both at once, never one at the cost of the other.
- **The business owner is the hero.** Speak to their outcome (looking legitimate, being found, winning customers), not Kazoom's features. They are the protagonist; Kazoom is the helper.
- **Earn trust in the open.** Clear claims, real examples, plain pricing, no dark patterns. Every choice should counter the "cheap and risky" fear directly.
- **Clarity over cleverness.** The audience is non-technical and time-poor. Plain words, obvious next steps, no jargon. If a sentence needs decoding, cut it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA. AA contrast on all text and meaningful UI, full keyboard navigability, visible focus states, and honored `prefers-reduced-motion`. Copy written for a broad, non-technical, mobile-first audience.
